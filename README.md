# TotalCare Health System: Emergency Department Performance Dashboard

**Tool:** Microsoft Excel (Tables, PivotTables, PivotCharts, slicers, formulas)
**My role:** Healthcare Data Analyst
**Data:** 5,000 emergency department visits across five hospitals

## Business overview
TotalCare Health System is a Canadian healthcare organisation running hospitals across several communities in Ontario. It provides emergency, inpatient, outpatient, diagnostic and specialist care. This project focuses on the emergency departments of five hospitals, each serving a different patient population and facing different levels of demand, staffing, bed availability and patient-flow pressure.

## The business problem
TotalCare collects detailed ED data but had not analysed it in a way that showed where the biggest problems were. Management wanted to know why wait times were rising, which hospitals were most affected, and where to focus improvement efforts. In short: where are the major performance problems, what is contributing to them, and where should TotalCare act first?

## My role and approach
As the Healthcare Data Analyst, I was asked to standardise the ED dataset, calculate key healthcare KPIs, analyse patient-flow patterns and build an executive dashboard in Excel.

1. **Prepared the data:** converted the raw dataset into a structured Excel Table, checked data types, standardised categories, handled duplicates and added two new columns
2. **Analysed patient flow:** built PivotTables on a dedicated sheet covering visits by month, shift, triage level (CTAS), hospital, arrival method and reason for visit.
3. **Calculated KPIs:** total visits, patients not seen, average bed occupancy, average wait time, average patient satisfaction and total financial value.
4. **Built the dashboard:** designed a one-page executive view with PivotCharts and slicers (year and arrival method) so leadership can filter the results.

## Key findings
- **Overall performance:** 5,000 visits, with 246 patients (4.9%) leaving without being seen. Average wait time was 112 minutes, average bed occupancy 88.8% and average patient satisfaction 3.66.
- **Shift pressure:** the evening shift accounted for 159 of the 246 patients not seen (about 65%), compared with 62 on days and 25 on nights.
- **Acuity:** almost all patients who left were low-acuity. About 96% (235 of 246) were CTAS 4 or 5. Roughly 18% of CTAS 5 patients and 9% of CTAS 4 patients were not seen, compared with under 1% in CTAS 1 to 3.
- **Hospital variation:** the share of patients attended to ranged from 90.6% at Riverside General to 97.4% at Northgate Regional, a gap of about 7 percentage points. The other three hospitals sat between 95.2% and 96.3%.
- **Demand:** the top reasons for visits were injury or fracture (678), shortness of breath (544) and abdominal pain (487). Monthly volumes dipped in April and September and rose towards the end of the year.

## Recommendations
1. **Strengthen evening-shift capacity.** Review staffing and patient flow, since the evening shift accounts for roughly two-thirds of walkouts.
2. **Create a fast-track or minor-care stream for CTAS 4 and 5 patients.** These patients make up most walkouts and are the most likely to leave during long waits.
3. **Investigate Riverside General.** It has the lowest attendance rate, so review its staffing, bed availability and flow, and learn from Northgate Regional's results.
4. **Track occupancy and wait time together by hospital and shift.** With occupancy near 89% and waits above 110 minutes, leadership should check whether the busiest periods line up with the longest waits and the most walkouts.

## What I learned
This project strengthened my skills in PivotTables, KPI design and dashboard layout. It also showed me how to turn a set of charts into clear, prioritised recommendations for non-technical decision-makers.
